About

Why this exists

An original benchmark of free-tier LLMs across five everyday builder tasks, plus a routing advisor built on the results, published as a quarterly-refreshed public index. The zero-budget constraint is the thesis, not a limitation — nobody has rigorously published what you can actually ship on a free tier.

Who this is for

Solo builders who are pre-revenue, students building portfolio work, and early-stage teams justifying a first spend. If you're choosing between a paid API and a free tier for a real project, this index is measuring the question you actually have.

What it does and doesn't claim

It measures four free-tier models — Gemini, Groq, Mistral, and a local Ollama model — across summarization, extraction, classification, RAG Q&A, and structured JSON output, and ships a client-side advisor that filters and ranks them against your stated workload. It does not publish an overall ranking or a single "best model" — the right model depends on your volume, latency need, and error tolerance, and a leaderboard would flatten exactly the tradeoff this index exists to show. It does not price anything — free-tier terms and paid pricing both move faster than this index refreshes, so it publishes the volume threshold where a tier's ceiling binds and names which paid path removes that constraint, unpriced, for you to check against the vendor's current page.

The judge behind every score here is uncalibrated — its held-out agreement with a careful human came in at κ=0.358, below this project's own 0.45 floor. That's disclosed on every page, not just this one; see Methodology for the complete account.

Refresh cadence

Quarterly. Free-tier terms, rate limits, and model versions change without notice, and a stale benchmark quietly misleads — a dated index that says so is the honest alternative to a one-off report that goes stale silently. If refresh cost ever proves unsustainable for a single-author project, the cadence drops to semi-annual and that change is stated publicly here rather than quietly missed.

How to contribute

Everything is published — the rubric, the test cases, the gold references, the judge's justifications, and the raw benchmark rows. If you think a score is wrong, the highest-value thing you can do is dispute it on the specific cell: open Results, click into the model × task you disagree with, and use the "Disagree with this score?" form right there — it's the same rubric anyone can check your reasoning against. Model and task requests for the next refresh go in the form below. There's also a persistent "Feedback" tab on every page (bottom right) for anything that doesn't fit one of the three contextual forms — it still attaches whatever you were looking at when you opened it, shown to you before you send it.

Feedback, privacy, and on-site tracking

Everything in this section is a promise about what this site collects from you as a visitor. It has nothing to do with the author's name, photo, and contact details published further down this page — those are the author's own choice to publish, not data gathered from anyone who visits.

Feedback — every placement (under a recommendation, on a disputed score, the roadmap form, and the persistent tab) posts to a Google Form / Sheet. Each submission carries: a timestamp; the route you were on; an anonymised, client-generated session ID that is not persisted across visits (it lives in sessionStorage and is gone when you close the tab); the advisor inputs or model/task cell behind whatever you were looking at; your free-text response; and the index version (generated_at from index.json) so a disputed score can be traced to the exact data it was scored under. It does not capture an account, a name, an email address, an IP-derived identity, or anything from a third-party analytics tool — there is no third-party tool in this pipeline at all.

Tracking — this is new, and it is a real change: previously nothing about a visit here was recorded unless you chose to submit feedback; now a small, first-party, self-made set of events records automatically as you use the site, with no action required from you and no per-event notice. Those events — advisor submissions and the input combination behind them, which recommendation was shown, filter-table opens, which results lens you're on, whether you reached this page or Methodology, and feedback opens versus completions — post to their own Google Form/Sheet (a separate form from feedback's, same idea), under the same anonymised, non-persisted session ID above. Nothing else — no mouse movement, no scroll depth, no fingerprinting, and nothing that follows you across a visit boundary. It fails silently and never blocks the page: events queue locally and send in one batch when you navigate away, and if the request fails for any reason, the site behaves exactly as if tracking were switched off.
